Weather Fade Research

Week-ahead, month-ahead and quarter-ahead weather fades for UK power and NBP gas · built 16 Oct 2026 00:35 · nothing here is financial advice

How to read this

  • Paper trading only. No real money; trades are simulated on historical prices, after an allowance for trading costs.
  • Two periods. Every setting was chosen using the tuning period only. The test period was never used to pick anything, so it is the honest estimate of how the strategy would do.
  • t-stat says how unlikely a result is to be luck: around 2 or more is hard to get by chance; below 1 could easily be noise.
  • Sharpe is profit per unit of risk, per year: 0.5 is decent for a single strategy, 1+ is excellent.

The short version

  • Week-ahead fades (power and gas) don't pay. Wind forecasts are reliable out to about 5 days, so a market that believes them is mostly right; only days 6-7 need pulling back, and that is too small after costs.
  • The forecasts are still valuable for power: our weather-based fair price predicts next week's power price far better than recent prices (about £14 vs £17.5/MWh miss).
  • Gas barely responds to the week-ahead weather compared with everything else that moves it.
  • The month/quarter-ahead fade is promising. Betting that recent unusual weather won't carry into next month beat a spot-anchored price on all four products (power and gas, month and quarter) in the test period. How much real forward prices anchor on spot is the open question.

UK power: week-ahead weather fade

No signal

Bet that the market over-trusts 5-7 day wind forecasts: price next week from the forecast pulled back toward normal by how reliable it has been, and trade any gap to the market price.

What we found. The weather does predict next week's power price well. Our fair price misses by about £14/MWh, against £17.5 for 'same as last week'. But the fade itself (betting against a market that believes the forecast) found almost nothing to fade. Wind forecasts turn out to be trustworthy out to 4-5 days; only days 6 and 7 need pulling back (trust factor 0.87 and 0.69). Averaged over a whole Mon-Sun week, that pull-back rarely moves the price by more than the £2/MWh needed to trade. In the test year the pure fade made no trades on the week product, and lost on single days 6 days out (t = -2.1). The big profits in the table come from markets that partly ignore the forecast, which a real market doesn't do much. Verdict: no tradeable fade. The useful thing built here is the fair-value model: compared against a real broker quote, it would show whether the market ever drifts from the forecast.

How it works

  1. Every Sunday at 10pm, take the European, US and German weather models' wind forecasts for 8 GB wind-farm areas for each of the next 7 days (only forecasts already published by then).
  2. Turn wind speeds into a GB wind output forecast with a learned power curve (checked against Elexon metered wind).
  3. Measure how reliable forecasts have been at each lead time: 1 day ahead is almost perfect, 7 days ahead needs pulling ~40% back toward normal.
  4. Convert the pulled-back wind forecast into a price with a model of how GB prices react to wind and weekday (the 'fair' price).
  5. Compare with the market price. If the market trusts the raw forecast, fair differs from it whenever the forecast is extreme: that difference is the fade.
  6. Trade 1 MW if the gap is at least £2/MWh; settle against the actual average price of the delivery days; £1/MWh cost.

Size: 1 MW. Tuning period ends 2025-09-01; everything after is the untouched test.

Things to be aware of

  • Real week-ahead quotes are not free, so the market price is simulated (0-100% trust in the forecast). The real market is somewhere in that range - the results show what each case would earn.
  • Decisions are on Sunday night because the free forecast archive stops at 7 days; the real week-ahead contract mostly trades Monday-Friday of the week before.
  • About 2.5 years of forecast history: the holdout year is ~55 weeks, so results are noisy.
  • Settles against the APX market index, a stand-in for the official week-ahead settlement.

NBP gas: week-ahead weather fade

No signal

Same idea as the power fade, for gas: forecast cold or calm weeks push gas prices up; we price next week from the forecast pulled back toward normal and trade any gap.

What we found. Weather barely helps predict next week's gas price. The weather model's error is 10.5p/therm against 10.6 for 'same as last week' in the test year. The model learned sensible effects (colder weeks, especially on the Continent, raise prices), but they are small next to everything else that moves gas: LNG cargoes, Norway, storage, geopolitics. The pure fade never found a gap worth trading. Verdict: no signal. NBP is more liquid than UK power, but liquidity doesn't help if the edge isn't there.

How it works

  1. Every Sunday at 10pm, take 1-7 day temperature forecasts for 4 GB cities and 4 Continental cities (Berlin, Paris, Amsterdam, Milan) and the GB wind forecast.
  2. Turn them into heating degree days (how far below 15.5°C each day is) and a GB wind capacity factor.
  3. A price model learned on 2018-2026 history says how gas prices move when next week is colder / calmer than the last 7 days.
  4. Pull each forecast back toward normal by how reliable it has been at that lead time, and price next week with it ('fair').
  5. Compare with the market price; trade if the gap is at least 1.5p/therm; settle against the actual average System Average Price (SAP) of the delivery days.
  6. Size: 25,000 therms per day of delivery (25 standard lots), 0.5p/therm cost.

Size: 25,000 therms/day. Tuning period ends 2025-09-01; everything after is the untouched test.

Things to be aware of

  • Market price is simulated (0-100% trust in the forecast), as real week-ahead NBP quotes are not free.
  • SAP is the National Gas balancing-market average, close to but not the same as NBP day-ahead.
  • Gas prices react strongly to non-weather news (LNG, Norway, storage, geopolitics), which this ignores.
  • About 2.5 years of forecast history: ~55 holdout weeks.

Power & gas: month/quarter-ahead weather fade

Promising, unproven

Bet that recent unusual weather does not carry into next month: price the next month or quarter from the weather-neutral level and normal weather, and trade against a price anchored on recent spot.

What we found. The longer-term fade works on a different principle. Weather can't be forecast a month out, but unusual weather also doesn't persist, so next month should look like a normal month at today's weather-neutral price, not like the last two weeks. That held up. Our fair price was usually more accurate than recent spot: for gas quarters it missed by 36% against 43-49% in tuning and 18% against 19-20% in the test; for power months 18% against 19% vs the 14-day average in the test. Against a market fully anchored on recent spot, the fade made money in the test period on all four products (power month: 37 trades, 62% winners, t = 2.3). Against a market only half-anchored, results were mixed and sometimes lost. Verdict: promising, unproven. The direction is consistent, but the profit depends on how much real month-ahead prices lean on recent spot, which only real quotes can show. Few data points: 89 months, 28 quarters.

How it works

  1. On the 24th of each month, look at the last 2-4 weeks of spot prices and the weather behind them.
  2. Strip the weather out to get a weather-neutral price level (power: wind/cold/weekday model; gas: cold in GB and the Continent, and GB wind).
  3. Price next month (or quarter) at that level with normal weather for the time of year: normal wind for that month and normal temperature for each date, from history only.
  4. A market anchored on recent spot quotes something closer to recent spot. If our fair price differs by 2% or more, trade toward fair.
  5. Settle against the actual average spot price over the delivery month or quarter.
  6. Size: £100k of power or gas at the entry price; costs £1/MWh (power) or 0.5p/therm (gas).

Size: £100k position per trade. Tuning period ends 2023-01-01; everything after is the untouched test.

Things to be aware of

  • Real month/quarter-ahead quotes are not free: the market price is simulated as anchored 50% or 100% on the last 14 or 28 days of spot.
  • Monthly decisions mean few data points: ~49 months tuning (Dec 2018-Dec 2022, including the 2021-22 crisis) and ~45 months test; quarters have a quarter as many.
  • Fuel-price trends (e.g. gas in 2021-22) move monthly prices far more than weather does, and this strategy has no view on them.
  • Settles against spot averages (daily APX index / SAP), a stand-in for the real contracts' settlement.
